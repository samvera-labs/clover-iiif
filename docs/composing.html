Composing Layouts

Composing Layouts

Every template is a composition of Clover's parts, and so is a layout of your own. A Root loads the content and holds where the reader is. Every part inside it reads from the nearest Root, wherever it sits in your markup, so a layout is your own HTML with parts placed in it.

Composing with Clover 3.x, @samvera/clover-iiif? See Clover 3.x › Composing.

A layout of your own

The parts are named exports. import * as Clover gives them dot notation, and they tree-shake: a page ships only the parts it renders. Here the title, position and buttons sit beside the stage.

import * as Clover from "@nulib/clover";
import "@nulib/clover/styles.css";
 
<Clover.Root iiifContent={url} className="my-layout">
  <Clover.Content />
  <aside>
    <Clover.Label as="h3" />
    <Clover.Label of="canvas" />
    <Clover.Position />
    <Clover.Previous />
    <Clover.Next />
  </aside>
</Clover.Root>;

Start from a template

The templates are built only from these parts, so a template's source is a good place to start. Copy Default.tsx and Default.css from packages/clover/src/templates/default/ (or another template's folder) into your app, and read ../../parts in its imports as @nulib/clover. Rename its clover-template-default-* classes to your own, and change what you like: nothing in a template is private.

To keep the Viewer's shell, pass your layout as its template; it takes the Root props like any template:

import Viewer, * as Clover from "@nulib/clover";
 
function MyLayout(props) {
  return (
    <Clover.Root {...props}>
      <Clover.Content />
      <Clover.Pager />
    </Clover.Root>
  );
}
 
<Viewer iiifContent={url} template={MyLayout} />;

iiifContent can be a Manifest, a Collection, a Canvas or a content state, as a URL or an object; see what the Viewer opens. Every template, and the Viewer, takes these props and hands them to its Root.

Parts

PartWhat it renders
RootLoads the content and holds where the reader is. Renders a div (or as), and announces moves.
ContentThe current view, drawn by Canvas: a page, a spread, a video or sound. Children join its controls. While it loads, Clover's loading mark runs along its top edge; nothing moves.
LabelThe Manifest's label, or the current Canvas's with of="canvas", in the reader's language.
Previous, NextRound arrow controls that move a view at a time (a spread, in a paged book), as Items' arrows do. Each names itself on hover and focus, and is hidden on the first or last view.
Position"2 / 5", counted in views; "Item 2 of 5" to assistive technology, in the reader's language.
PagerPrevious, Position and Next in a nav.
StatusLoading, as Clover's loading mark (announced "Loading…"), or what went wrong. Nothing once loaded. loading={false} only announces it, where Content shows it.
ItemsThumbnails of the views, as a carousel, list or grid. See Items.
InformationPanelTabs of information. About by default; give it Tabs to choose your own.
TabOne tab of the panel: value, label, and what it shows. Your own components go here.
AboutEverything the Manifest says about itself, as 3.x's About tab shows it.
PanelToggleShows and hides the panel. As a child of Content, it sits with the Canvas's controls.
AnnotationsThe view's annotations by set: content to read, a button to find each on the Canvas, a switch per set.
DownloadThe rendering of the page in view and of the Manifest (a PDF, say), as links behind one button.
TranscriptThe view's captions as text: the current cue marked as it plays, a press seeking there.
ContentsThe table of contents from the Manifest's Ranges, the entry the reader is in marked.
SearchIIIF Content Search (1 or 2): a field, then the hits by page with the words around them.
CollectionThe Collection the Manifest is in: its label, summary, members, homepage and address, as the InformationPanel's Collection section shows it. Fetched when it first shows. headingLevel sets its headings (default 3).
CollectionSelectIn a Collection, a menu of its members; choosing one shows it.
CanvasThe renderer itself. Inside a Root it shows the current view; see Canvas.
TipA control's popover: its name and its key, on hover and focus. See below.

Parts that have nothing to show render nothing: Pager for a single view, Label with no label.

Properties

Each IIIF descriptive property is a part of its own, reading the Manifest, or the current Canvas with of="canvas". A value passed as value wins, so they also work outside a Root. Text is in the reader's language and marked with lang; HTML is limited to what IIIF allows and sanitised, by a sanitiser that loads only when a value carries markup.

PartProperty
Labellabel (takes label rather than value)
Summarysummary, cut to five lines with "Read more" (below)
Metadatametadata, as a description list; renderValue to customise
RequiredStatementrequiredStatement
Rightsrights, as a link
Homepage, Rendering, SeeAlso, PartOfLinked resources, by label, with their format
Thumbnailthumbnail (or a Canvas's first image), at size
IdThe resource's id, as a link

Every summary Clover shows is Summary, cut to five lines with a "Read more" button under it when it runs longer, and "Read less" to cut it again. The number of lines is a CSS custom property, --clover-summary-lines, set on .clover-summary or anything around it; none shows it whole:

.my-layout .clover-summary {
  --clover-summary-lines: 3;
}

ReadMore gives text of your own the same button: wrap one element (or a component that forwards its ref) and clamp it in your CSS; it carries data-read-more="open" while open.

<Clover.ReadMore>
  <p className="my-note">{note}</p>
</Clover.ReadMore>

The information panel

InformationPanel shows the sections that have something to show: About, and Collection, Annotations, Contents, Search, Transcript and Map when the content has them. Collection, after About, lists the members of the Collection the Manifest was reached through, or the one it is partOf (fetched only when the section opens), and choosing one shows it. It lays them out as tabs (layout="tabs", the default) or stacked boxes (layout="accordion"), from the same parts and the same state, so switching keeps what is open. value, defaultValue and onValueChange choose the open section.

Give it Tabs to choose the sections yourself. Each part a default section uses (About, Annotations, Contents, Search, Transcript) works on its own too, in a Tab or anywhere in the layout.

<Clover.InformationPanel>
  <Clover.Tab value="about" label="About">
    <Clover.About />
  </Clover.Tab>
  <Clover.Tab value="page" label="This page">
    <Clover.Label of="canvas" as="h3" />
    <Clover.Summary of="canvas" />
    <Clover.Metadata of="canvas" />
  </Clover.Tab>
</Clover.InformationPanel>

PanelToggle shows and hides it; as a child of Content, it sits with the Canvas's controls. Give the panel onClose and it gets a close button. Closing from inside it hands focus back to its PanelToggle (whatever names it in aria-controls). Add closeOnEscape when the panel floats over the page rather than sitting beside it.

Panes side by side

Split, SplitPane and SplitHandle put panes side by side, with a bar between them the reader drags. One pane beside each bar has a size (with min and max, in px, rem or %); the other takes what is left. The bar is a focusable separator: the arrow keys move it (Shift for bigger steps), Home and End go to the limits, and a double click puts the pane back to its size. With storageKey the reader's sizes are remembered. The Default and Document templates use it, and nothing ties it to any part.

<Clover.Root iiifContent={url}>
  <Clover.Split storageKey="my-layout">
    <Clover.SplitPane min="16rem">
      <Clover.Content />
    </Clover.SplitPane>
    <Clover.SplitHandle />
    <Clover.SplitPane size="24rem" min="14rem">
      <Clover.InformationPanel />
    </Clover.SplitPane>
  </Clover.Split>
</Clover.Root>

Parts of your own

Hooks read from the nearest Root. Each re-renders only when what it reads changes.

HookReturns
useStatus(){ status, error }
useManifest()The Manifest, in Presentation 4.
useSequence(){ canvases, views, direction }
useVisibleCanvases()The Canvases of the current view.
useActiveCanvas()The Canvas the reader is on.
useNavigation(){ position, total, hasPrevious, hasNext, previous, next, first, last, goTo }
useLocalized(value){ text, lang } for a IIIF language map, in the Root's language.
function PageCount() {
  const { position, total } = Clover.useNavigation();
  return (
    <span>
      {position + 1} / {total}
    </span>
  );
}

useClover() gives { clover }, the Root's state and actions (clover.goTo(canvasId), clover.next(), clover.search(query) and the rest), for parts that act rather than read.

Controls of your own

Every control Clover draws as an icon alone names itself in a popover on hover and on keyboard focus, with its keyboard shortcut when it has one. Give a control of your own the same: its name as aria-label, its key (if it has one) as aria-keyshortcuts, and Tip inside it, last, with the same words. Tip is hidden from assistive technology, which reads the control's own name, and hides on Escape.

<button type="button" aria-label="Bookmark" onClick={save}>
  <BookmarkIcon aria-hidden="true" />
  <Clover.Tip label="Bookmark" />
</button>

Clover's popups open one at a time: the Canvas's information and its menus (annotations, Choice, an animation's frames), the playback controls' menus and Download's list. Opening one closes whichever other is open, however it was opened. A popup of your own joins them with useOneOpen, given its open state and the way to close it:

const [open, setOpen] = useState(false);
Clover.useOneOpen(open, () => setOpen(false));

Renderers of your own

Give Root (or one Content) renderers, and a view they claim is drawn by your component instead of the Canvas: a 3D model, a PDF, anything Clover does not draw. Claim a view by its Canvas id, by a painted body's format, or with a match test. The first renderer that claims a view draws it. This is 3.x's customDisplays.

function ModelViewer({ bodies }) {
  // Inside the Root: Clover's hooks work here (useNavigation, useActiveCanvas, useClover…).
  return <model-viewer src={bodies[0].id} camera-controls />;
}
 
<Clover.Root
  iiifContent={url}
  renderers={[{ format: ["model/gltf-binary"], component: ModelViewer }]}
>
  <Clover.Content />
</Clover.Root>;

A renderer gets the view's canvases, their painting bodies (a Choice as its first item, a SpecificResource as its source) and Content's children. The Viewer and every template pass renderers through to their Root.

Styles

Import @nulib/clover/styles.css once. Its rules sit in @layer clover, so your own CSS wins without a fight over specificity. Without it, every part still renders a plain, usable layout.

The stage takes its height from --clover-content-height (default 60vh):

.my-layout {
  --clover-content-height: 70vh;
}

Every region keeps --clover-inset (default 1.618rem, the scale's l) between its edge and its content: the header and thumbnail strip, the information panel, the picture on the stage at home. Inside a region, each level is a step less: its parts 1rem apart, what is within a part 0.618rem. Set it once for a tighter or roomier layout:

.my-layout {
  --clover-inset: var(--clover-space-m);
}

The picture fills its stage edge to edge at home; --clover-canvas-inset keeps a margin round it instead. The Canvas's controls sit --clover-canvas-chrome-inset in from its edges, the region's inset by default, so they line up with what is round the stage.

Clover sets no font and steps every space from your 1rem, so a layout reads at your page's type and size. Its colours are custom properties (--clover-color-accent and the rest): set one on any ancestor and it cascades. The round controls have two of their own, a dark fill (--clover-color-control) and white glyphs (--clover-color-control-glyph), and keep them in a dark mode: they sit over a Canvas's images, so they do not swap with the page's text and background colours. Each part names itself with one clover-* class on its root and states what it is doing in data-* attributes (data-media on a Canvas playing video or sound, data-layout on the panel's sections), for your own CSS to select.
